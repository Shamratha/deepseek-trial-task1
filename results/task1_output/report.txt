DESTINATION PERFORMANCE — MANAGEMENT EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (FY2025)

Overview
- Total bookings: 116,620 with gross revenue of $162.30M across 12 destinations and 12 months.
- Revenue per booking averages $1,392; the portfolio generates $6.64 of revenue for every $1 of
  marketing spend, with refund exposure of 2.7% of revenue and cancellation risk at 6.4% of bookings.

Growth and Profitability
- The market is meaningfully seasonal: monthly revenue peaks in April ($18.0M) and March ($17.0M),
  troughs October ($9.4M), and recovers modestly in December ($11.5M). Booking volumes follow the
  same shape (10.0k Jan -> 12.7k Apr -> 6.7k Oct -> 8.3k Dec).
- Highest-yield destinations by revenue per booking: Vancouver ($1,878), Cape Town ($1,786),
  Singapore ($1,639), and New York ($1,571). These justify premium positioning.
- Highest absolute revenue contributors: Vancouver ($24.0M), Singapore ($19.3M), and Queenstown
  ($18.9M). Together they account for 38% of total revenue, making them the growth anchors.
- Paris underperforms on yield ($919 per booking) with the lowest bookings (5,176) and highest
  marketing intensity (24.8% of revenue), signaling an efficiency problem rather than demand.

Refund and Cancellation Risk
- Refund rates vary ~2.3x across destinations: Queenstown (3.8%), Barcelona (3.7%) and New York
  (3.6%) are the highest; Paris, Cape Town, and Singapore are the lowest (1.6%).
- Cancellation rates tell the same story — Barcelona (8.5%), New York (8.6%) and Queenstown (8.3%)
  cancel at roughly double the rate of Cape Town (4.1%), Singapore (4.0%), or Paris (3.9%).
- Estimated exposure: $4.37M in refunds and 7,438 lost bookings annually. Prioritizing booking
  mechanics (deposits, flexible rebooking windows) in Queenstown, Barcelona, and New York could
  recover 0.8-1.0 points of revenue margin.

Marketing Efficiency
- Portfolio marketing spend totals $24.4M (15.0% of revenue); the blended ratio is efficient but
  uneven.
- Star performers: Vancouver (10.8% of revenue), Cape Town (12.0%), Singapore (12.7%).
- Underperformers: Paris (24.8%), Rio de Janeiro (21.4%), Tokyo (19.4%), Marrakech (18.3%) — all
  above 18% of revenue, suggesting either early-stage demand building or spend leakage.
- Reallocating 5 points of marketing mix from Paris/Rio/Tokyo to Cape Town/Vancouver/Singapore
  would redirect roughly $1.6-2.4M annually toward higher-yield, lower-risk routes.

Customer Experience
- Ratings are generally healthy (4.27-4.60). Cape Town (4.27) and Vancouver (4.28) trail the
  portfolio despite strong yield, signaling experience gaps that may cap repeat business.
- Trip lengths vary from 3.5 (Singapore) to 5.6 days (New York); long-haul, high-yield routes
  (Vancouver 5.1, New York 5.6) support price premiums but also raise cancellation exposure.

Tradeoffs and Risks
- Revenue concentration: Vancouver + Singapore + Queenstown = 38% of revenue; a disruption in any
  one (weather, air capacity, geopolitics) hits the portfolio disproportionately.
- High-yield, low-rating pairs (Cape Town, Vancouver) risk losing share if experience quality is
  not addressed before competitors invest.
- Seasonal trough (Sep-Oct) creates cash-flow and staffing risk; shoulder-season campaigns and
  off-peak pricing should be evaluated before the peak-year planning cycle.

Recommendations (highest-leverage actions)
1. Shift marketing to Vancouver, Cape Town, Singapore; cap Paris/Rio/Tokyo spend until efficiency
   improves.
2. Target refund/cancellation behavior in Queenstown, Barcelona, New York with deposit and
   rebooking policy changes (est. +0.8-1.0 pt margin).
3. Protect the 3 growth anchors against concentration risk via inventory and airlift planning.
4. Fix experience gaps in Cape Town and Vancouver (rating uplift of +0.3 would support premium
   pricing and repeat travel).
